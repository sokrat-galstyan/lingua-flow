# Learning Methodology and Adaptation

## Guiding approach

Combine explicit language instruction with comprehensible meaningful input, meaningful output, deliberate fluency work, retrieval practice, spaced review and ongoing corrections. Use the Four Strands framework (language-focused learning, meaning-focused input, meaning-focused output, fluency development) as a **cycle-level balance heuristic**, not an inflexible rule for every lesson.

Allocate extra attention to the learner’s reported or evidenced weak points; do not assume a particular weakness. Every week must still contain genuine reading, listening, writing, speaking, pronunciation and natural-expression opportunities. Never wait to finish all grammar before beginning production.

## Grammar skill progression

1. **Understand:** clear rule + contrasting examples + a way to check understanding.
2. **Recognize:** choose or explain correct forms in genuine context.
3. **Controlled production:** complete transformations and guided sentences.
4. **Independent production:** express novel ideas in original writing / speech.
5. **Automaticity:** repeated retelling, timed reformulation and conversation where form selection becomes faster.
6. **Transfer and return:** review in later units with different vocabulary / communication contexts.

Keep three distinct signals: conceptual understanding, accuracy in original language, and automaticity when time-constrained. One correct exercise does not establish all three.

## Vocabulary and naturalness

Build useful expressions, common collocations and multiword chunks **in context**. Use brief retrieval prompts, such as Anki cards with whole phrases and example sentences. Avoid overloading lessons with decontextualized terms; 5–8 items is a common reasonable range, not a compulsory quota. Include register distinctions (casual, neutral, formal) and American/British alternatives where relevant.

## Input activities

- Listening: first pass for meaning; second for details; then transcript if available; short recap. Provide captions/transcripts when possible, but do not force transcript-first listening every time.
- Reading: skim for gist, reread for details, notice useful patterns, summarize. Choose diverse accessible topics, avoiding unnecessary specialist notation.
- Mix graded materials and authentic content when comprehension supports it.

## Output and feedback

- Writing: set audience/purpose, short target, grammar focus. First draft -> learner self-edit -> automated feedback when necessary -> revision. Label grammatical errors separately from stylistic alternatives; AI may be wrong.
- Speaking: initial short descriptions and retelling, then independent recordings/shadowing, later AI voice conversations with delayed correction, ultimately real human conversations when suitable.
- Speaking tool prompts should include topic, target structure, duration, pacing, and when to correct. No dedicated microphone required.
- Human practice isn't a default prerequisite; introduce it to verify real conversational transfer and social pragmatics, or for feedback that tools can't reliably provide.

## Fluency versus accuracy

Accuracy activities allow deliberate thinking and correction. Fluency activities prioritize continuous meaning-making (familiar topics, reduced pauses, manageable speed), with error review **after** the activity. Both are necessary.

## Scheduling and review

Use configured session duration and weekly frequency; review sessions count toward the schedule. Optional enjoyable input is outside focused work. Distribute early review across days and a longer weekly consolidation session. Use early and later retrieval revisits (e.g., next day, later in the week, following weeks) flexibly; do not pretend an exact universal spacing interval is scientifically guaranteed.

## Adaptation decisions

- **Understood but hesitant:** increase timed production and repeated spontaneous use.
- **Incorrect despite understanding:** increase contrastive contextual practice and feedback.
- **Doesn't understand:** re-teach the relevant rule from a reputable source with worked contrasts.
- **Too easy:** enrich input and output, not merely add hours.
- **Overwhelming:** shorten texts, reduce new concepts, redistribute work across cycles.
- **One weak domain:** add more targeted activities while keeping other skills progressing.

## Assessment policy

Optional formal tests are separate check-ins, not curriculum gates or finish lines. Avoid claims of CEFR progress without appropriate evidence. Use real samples (writing, speaking, comprehension, exercises) plus learner observations, but label uncertainty. Do not require a rigid test to begin cycle 001.
